Dynamic Energy Simulation with UBEM (UMI)

Dynamic energy modelling of a small building, run for two contrasting Italian climates to show how climate flips the design priorities — and how the envelope should respond.

UMIUBEMDynamic SimulationBuilding PhysicsEnergy EfficiencyEnvelope Retrofit

Polytechnic of Milan — Advanced Building Physics · Group 16 · Two case studies: Villafranca di Verona & Palermo Boccadifalco

Overview

This project applies Urban Building Energy Modelling (UBEM) through a bottom-up dynamic simulation in UMI to a small hypothetical building. For each climate the workflow is the same: quantify the monthly heating and cooling demand, and redesign the envelope stratigraphy to cut the dominant need by at least 15%.

The key insight comes from running it in two different climates: the dominant energy need — and therefore the whole optimisation strategy — flips between them.

Building & model setup (shared)

Adiabatic floor, 3 m height, a south window (40% WWR) and a west window (30% WWR). The envelope combines hollow-brick and concrete-panel walls with a single-glazing wood frame in the base case. The retrofit acts on the roof and wall build-up of the main, south-facing space, and all results below refer to that space.

Two Climates Compared

Same building, same method — opposite problem. Verona's continental climate is heating-driven; Palermo's Mediterranean climate is cooling-driven.

The envelope upgrade (roof insulation + cavity-wall insulation) sharply cuts heating demand in both climates. The difference is what matters next: in Palermo, heating almost disappears but cooling remains the dominant need, so insulation alone does not address the main energy demand there.
🏔️Villafranca di VeronaHeating-driven

In Verona's continental climate, heating dominates: in the base case the annual heating need (about 2,196 kWh) is roughly twice the cooling need (about 1,015 kWh). Cooling is much lower than in Palermo (about 1,608 kWh), because Verona's summers are cooler.

Envelope optimisation — heating focus

Replacing the wall insulation with high-performance cavity insulation and adding a roof insulation layer (absent in the base case) brought the roof U-value from 1.46 to 0.34 W/m²K and the wall from 0.57 to 0.36 W/m²K — a ~52% cut in heating demand (2,196 → 1,049 kWh).

🌞Palermo BoccadifalcoCooling-driven

In Palermo's Mediterranean climate the picture inverts: mild winters mean little heating, while hot summers and a long cooling season make cooling the dominant need: in the base case cooling (about 1,608 kWh) is nearly twice the heating need (about 908 kWh).

Envelope optimisation — cooling still dominant

After the retrofit, heating in Palermo almost disappears (from about 908 to 219 kWh a year, −76%), while cooling, although about 20% lower than in the base case, remains clearly dominant at about 1,293 kWh.

Conclusions

Insulation is highly effective against heating, but in a cooling-dominated climate it does not address the main energy need: in Palermo, cooling stays dominant even after the retrofit. A solution for reducing cooling needs is to work on the glazing, by introducing low-e glazing that limits summer solar gains. Across both climates, envelope measures must be tuned to the local climate, with solar control as important as insulation.
